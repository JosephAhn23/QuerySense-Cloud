Why random_page_cost matters

The problem: PostgreSQL defaults random_page_cost=4.0, which means random I/O (index scans) is priced at 4x sequential I/O. This was correct for HDDs where seeks took ~10ms vs 0.25ms for sequential reads.

On SSDs: Random and sequential reads are nearly the same speed. With random_page_cost=4.0, the planner avoids perfectly good indexes because it thinks they're 4x more expensive than they actually are.

The fix: Set random_page_cost=1.1 on SSDs. This alone can speed up queries 2-10x by encouraging index scans.
